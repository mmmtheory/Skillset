# Copy patterns

## Buttons and links

Verb plus object, specific to the action. "Save draft", "Send 3 invoices", "Delete account". Destructive buttons say what is destroyed. Links describe their destination: "See pricing", not "Click here". The confirm and cancel pair in a dialog: the confirm repeats the action from the title, the cancel is "Cancel" or "Keep editing".

## Errors

Three parts, as short as possible:

1. What happened, in the person's terms. "Your card was declined."
2. What it means for them, if not obvious. "You haven't been charged."
3. What to do. "Try another card or contact your bank."

Put the error next to the thing it's about. Don't blame ("You entered an invalid date"); describe ("Use a date after today"). No codes unless support needs them, and then after the human sentence.

## Empty states

Why it's empty, and the first step. "No projects yet. Create one to start inviting your team." First-use empties can teach; empties after a search or filter should help recover: "No results for 'invoce'. Check the spelling or clear filters."

## Success and confirmation

Confirm the outcome in a few words, with what happens next if anything. "Invoice sent to Aroha. You'll get a notification when it's paid." No celebration unless the brand and moment truly call for it (a first payment received, not a saved setting).

## Onboarding

One idea per screen. Tell people what they get, then what to do. Let them skip. Avoid explaining the interface they're looking at.

## Notifications

Lead with the news, then the name. "Payment received from Aroha: $240." Make it make sense on a lock screen with no context. Don't send copy that exists only to bring people back.

## Consent and permissions

Say what is collected or accessed, why, and what happens if they say no, before the system prompt. "We use your location to show nearby stores. You can still search by city if you'd rather not share it." The decline option is as clear as the accept.

## Destructive confirmations

Title asks the specific question: "Delete 'Q3 report'?" Body says what is lost and whether it can be undone. Button repeats the verb: "Delete report".

## Length

Write the shortest version that is still clear, then check it in the layout. Leave room for translation: German and Finnish often run 30 to 40 percent longer than English.
