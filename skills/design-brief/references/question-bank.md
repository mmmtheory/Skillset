# Question bank

Pick at most five, only where the answer would change the design direction. Always attach a proposed default.

Format each question like this:

> **Who is this mainly for?** I'd assume existing customers who already pay monthly, not new sign-ups. Right?

## Any project type

- What happens if we do nothing? (Reveals whether there is a real problem.)
- Who decides this is done, and what would make them say no?
- What has been tried before, and why did it stop?
- Is there a date that cannot move, and what is driving it?
- What must stay exactly as it is?

## New product or feature

- What are people doing today instead? (The real competitor is often a spreadsheet or a phone call.)
- How often will someone use this: once, weekly, all day?
- What has to be true for the first version to be worth shipping?
- Which part are we least sure people want?

## Redesign

- What is wrong with the current version, in the words of the people who use it?
- What works today that a redesign could accidentally break?
- Are we changing the look, the flow, or what the thing does?
- Do existing users need to keep their habits, or is a clean break acceptable?

## Optimisation

- Which metric, measured how, and what is it now?
- Where in the flow do we lose people, by our data, not by guess?
- What would we refuse to do even if it moved the number? (Dark patterns, hidden costs.)
- How long until we can read a result with confidence?

## Brand or campaign asset

- Where will this appear, at what size, next to what?
- What should someone think, feel or do after a three-second look?
- Which brand rules are fixed and which can flex here?
- Who is this definitely not for?

## Fix

- What exactly happens, for whom, how often?
- What is the cost of the defect: support tickets, lost revenue, risk?
- Is a stopgap acceptable while a proper fix is designed?

## Questions not to ask

- Anything already answered in the files or conversation.
- Preferences you can decide with a sensible default ("Do you want rounded corners?").
- Questions whose answers would not change the brief.
- Open-ended prompts with no default ("Tell me about your users").
