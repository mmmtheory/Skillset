# Weak and strong platforms from the same brief

**Brief:** "We're a coffee roaster in Wellington selling beans online and to about 40 cafes. Can you do our brand strategy?"

Context given: every bag carries the roast date and the farm; they ship within 24 hours of roasting; cafes get free barista training; a subscription is 60% of online revenue; they are small and cannot compete on price with supermarket brands.

## Weak

> **Purpose:** To bring people together over exceptional coffee.
>
> **Values:** Quality, passion, sustainability, community.
>
> **Positioning:** For coffee lovers, Kōwhai Roasters is the premium coffee brand that delivers an exceptional, ethically sourced experience.
>
> **Personality:** Warm, authentic, innovative.

What is wrong with it:

- Swap test: replace "Kōwhai Roasters" with any specialty roaster and every line still works.
- "Coffee lovers" is everyone who buys coffee. The cafes, 40 of them, vanish.
- "Ethically sourced" and "innovative" have no proof in the brief.
- The purpose changes no decision.
- Traits are adjectives with no behaviour.

## Strong

> **Positioning:** For people who brew at home and want cafe-fresh coffee without thinking about it, Kōwhai is the roaster that gets beans to the door within days of roasting, because every bag ships within 24 hours of the roast and is dated so you can check.
>
> **What we give up:** Supermarket shoppers looking for the lowest price per kilo. Flavoured and decaf blends we can't roast to the same standard.
>
> **Promise:** You always know how fresh it is and where it came from.
>
> **Proof points**
> - Roast date and farm on every bag (current practice)
> - Shipped within 24 hours of roasting (current practice)
> - Free barista training for cafe partners (current practice)
> - *Assumption:* subscribers stay because of freshness rather than price
>
> **Personality**
> - **Exact, never fussy.** Dates and farms on every bag. Brew guides give grams and seconds, not "a scoop".
> - **Generous with what we know, never preachy.** Cafe partners get training for free. Product pages explain a choice without judging anyone's grinder.
> - **Local, never parochial.** We say where we roast and name the farms we buy from.
>
> **Voice: say the number**
> - Do: "Roasted Tuesday. At your door by Friday."
> - Don't: "Freshly roasted with love and shipped fast!"
>
> **Words:** use "roasted on", "farm", "grams"; avoid "artisan", "premium", "passion".

Why it works: it fails the swap test for any competitor that does not date and ship within 24 hours, every claim is current practice or marked as an assumption, it names who it gives up, and a copywriter can write an email from the voice line alone.

Note: the cafe business is a secondary audience here. A strategy built for the cafes would be a different territory (the roaster that makes your baristas better). That is the kind of fork step 6 asks you to show, not average.
