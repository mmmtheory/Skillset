# BRAND.md template

The front matter is what other tools and agents read. The body explains the choices. Keep the whole file under about 1,200 words.

```markdown
---
brand: <Name>
version: 1
updated: <YYYY-MM-DD>
audience:
  core: <who, in what situation, trying to get what done>
  secondary: [<only if they shape the brand>]
frame: <what the brand is compared against>
positioning: >
  For <audience>, <brand> is the <frame> that <difference>, because <proof>.
promise: <what people can count on, every time>
personality:
  - trait: <trait>
    limit: <never ...>
voice:
  - principle: <principle>
    do: "<a line that does it>"
    dont: "<a line that breaks it>"
words:
  use: [<words and phrases the brand owns>]
  avoid: [<words the brand never uses>]
---

# <Name> brand platform

## Positioning
The statement, then one paragraph on why this position and not the obvious alternative.

## Audience
Core audience in their situation. What they are trying to get done, what gets in the way, what they use today.

## What we give up
Who this brand is happy to lose, and which reasonable requests it will say no to.

## Purpose
Only if true and decision-changing. Otherwise delete this section.

## Promise
One sentence, then two examples of what keeping it looks like in practice.

## Proof points
- Fact the business can show today, with its source
- *Assumption:* belief not yet confirmed

## Personality
For each trait: the trait, its limit, and two behaviours that show it in product, service or copy.

## Voice
For each principle: what it means, a line that does it, a line that breaks it.

## Words
Use / avoid, with a reason for any non-obvious entry.

## Existing equity
For a rebrand: what people already associate with the brand, and what to keep, change or drop.

## Open questions
- Question (owner)
```

## Field notes

**Frame of reference.** Choose the frame that makes the difference meaningful. A tea shop compared with other tea shops may have no difference; compared with the office coffee machine, it might.

**Promise versus positioning.** Positioning is where the brand stands relative to alternatives. The promise is what a customer can count on after choosing it.

**Traits with limits.** The limit stops the trait from turning into its vice. Playful, never at the customer's expense. Direct, never curt. Expert, never jargon.

**Behaviours.** A trait is only real if it changes something: how support answers, what the empty state says, which features ship, which ones do not.
