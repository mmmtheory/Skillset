# Strong and weak records

## Decision

Weak: "After a productive discussion, we aligned on moving forward with the new navigation."

Strong: "The mobile app uses a bottom tab bar with four tabs (Home, Pay, Rewards, Account). The hamburger menu is removed."

The strong version can be checked against the product. The weak one cannot.

## Options

Weak: "Option A: the new design (better). Option B: do nothing (worse)."

Strong: each option has the case its supporters would make. "Keep the hamburger menu. For: no re-learning for 40,000 existing users; room for more than five destinations. Against: Rewards, our second most used area, is two taps deep."

## Evidence

Weak: "Users prefer tab bars." (Which users? Says who?)

Strong: "**Research:** 4 of 5 participants found Rewards without help with tabs; 1 of 5 with the menu. [Prototype test, 12 Oct]" and "**Judgement:** The brand lead thinks five tabs crowd the illustrations. (Brand lead)"

Label opinions as judgement. They count, but readers need to know they are opinions.

## Trade-offs

Weak: none listed.

Strong: "We give up a home for low-traffic settings, which move into Account. Support expects a short rise in 'where did X go' contacts for the first month."

## Revisit when

Weak: "We'll revisit if needed." / "Revisit later."

Strong: "Revisit if Account tab visits rise above 30% of sessions (people hunting for moved items), or if we add a fifth main area."

## Dissent

Weak: leaving it out because it is awkward.

Strong: "The Android lead preferred a navigation drawer for consistency with Material guidance. Agreed to commit; asked that we revisit if Android retention drops after release."

## Decider

Weak: "The team."

Strong: "Decider: Head of Product. Consulted: design, iOS and Android leads, support."
