# Choosing a method

## Match the question to the method

| The team needs to know | Use | Not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can people complete this flow, and where do they struggle? | Task-based usability test, moderated if the prototype is rough or the flow is complex | A survey asking if it looks easy |
| Why people do what the analytics show | Moderated test or contextual interview | A/B test alone |
| Where people expect to find things | Tree test (structure) or card sort (grouping) | A full prototype |
| Where people click first | First-click test | Interviews |
| What a page communicates at a glance | Five-second test | Long tasks |
| Whether people want this at all, and how they solve it today | Interviews about past behaviour; a fake door or concierge test | A usability test of a prototype |
| What people would pay | Pricing research or a live price test | Asking "how much would you pay?" in a session |
| Which of two designs performs better, with numbers | A/B test, or a larger unmoderated study sized for it | Five moderated sessions |
| Whether it works with assistive technology | Sessions with people who use screen readers, magnifiers or switch access, plus an expert audit | Simulating it yourself only |

## Moderated or unmoderated

- **Moderated**: you can probe, recover from prototype dead ends and follow surprises. Slower, five to eight sessions in a few days. Best for early or complex work.
- **Unmoderated**: faster and cheaper, bigger samples, no moderator bias. Tasks must be self-explanatory and the prototype robust. Best for checking a near-final flow or comparing simple tasks.

## Fidelity

- Paper or low fidelity: concepts, structure and wording. People critique less and act more when it looks unfinished.
- Clickable prototype: flows and navigation. Every path in a task must work or have a planned dead end.
- Coded or live: interaction details, real data, performance, accessibility.

## Sample size

- Finding problems in a flow: five to eight participants per distinct group (new versus existing customers count as separate groups if they will use it differently). Run in rounds: test five, fix, test again.
- Estimating a completion rate or time on task with a useful margin: twenty or more per group, usually unmoderated.
- Comparing two designs with confidence: size it like an experiment; a handful of sessions will not do it.

Report qualitative findings as counts ("4 of 6"), never as percentages.

## Session length

- Moderated remote: 45 to 60 minutes, with at least 10 minutes of buffer.
- Unmoderated: 15 to 20 minutes; drop-off rises sharply after that.
- Budget about 5 minutes for intro and consent, 5 for warm-up, 5 for wrap-up.
