# Writing tasks and questions

## Tasks

A task gives a goal, a reason and enough context to act, then gets out of the way.

| Weak | Better | Problem with the weak version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Click Send and add a new recipient. | Your sister in Fiji needs NZ$200 for rent by Friday. You haven't sent her money before. Send it to her. | Names the interface and the steps |
| Find the Rewards tab. | You've heard you earn points when you pay at cafes. Find out how many you have. | Uses the label people need to find |
| Explore the dashboard and tell us what you think. | You've just signed in after a week away. Check whether anything needs your attention. | No goal, no end point |
| Try to change your plan. | Your team has grown to 12 people and you've been told your plan only covers 10. Sort it out. | "Try" signals that it is hard |
| Book a table. | You're taking four friends out for dinner on Saturday at about 7. Book somewhere for all of you. | Missing the details people need to make choices |

Rules:

- Use the participant's words, not the product's. If the button says "Payees", the task says "someone you send money to".
- Give a reason and the details a real person would have (amount, date, who). Without them people invent details or stall.
- End at a recognisable point, so both you and the participant know when the task is over.
- One goal per task.
- Avoid "try", "explore", "find the X button", and verbs that describe the interface (click, tap, select, navigate, scroll).
- Put tasks in a realistic order and do not let one task set up the answer to the next.

## Questions

| Weak | Better | Problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Would you use this feature? | When did you last need to do this? What did you do? | Prediction; people are poor at forecasting their own behaviour |
| How easy and useful was that? | How easy or difficult was that? (then separately, if needed) What, if anything, would you use this for? | Double-barrelled |
| Didn't you find that confusing? | What did you expect to happen when you pressed that? | Leading |
| Do you like the new design? | What stood out to you on this screen? | Asks for an opinion people will give politely |
| How much would you pay for this? | (Do not ask in a usability test.) | Stated prices do not predict real behaviour |
| Why did you do that? | What were you looking for at that point? | "Why" can sound like blame and invites post-hoc reasons |

## Neutral probes

Use during tasks, sparingly:

- What are you looking for?
- What do you expect to happen?
- What's going through your mind?
- Tell me more about that.
- What would you do if I weren't here?

When asked "Is this right?" or "What should I click?", reply: "What would you do if you were on your own?" Do not explain the interface until the task is over.

## After each task

A single ease question (SEQ): "Overall, how easy or difficult was that task?" on a 1 to 7 scale, followed by "What made it [easy/difficult]?" The number is useful for comparing tasks; the follow-up is where the insight is.
