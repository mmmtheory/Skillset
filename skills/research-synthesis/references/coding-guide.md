# Coding guide

## Observation types

Tag each observation with one type. The type keeps interpretation out of the raw layer.

| Type | What it captures | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Behaviour | What they did or routinely do | P2 checks the bank app before every client invoice |
| Goal | What they are trying to achieve | P5 wants to know by Thursday whether payroll will clear |
| Pain | A cost they named or showed: time, money, worry, effort | P1 spends Sunday evenings reconciling receipts |
| Workaround | Something they built or adopted to cope | P4 keeps a paper list of which clients pay late |
| Reaction | Response to a stimulus in the session | P3 hesitated 10 seconds on the pricing screen, then asked what "seat" means |
| Context | Situation that shapes the rest | P6 works on a phone in a van between jobs |
| Suggestion | A solution the participant proposed | P2 asked for a "traffic light" for cash |

Suggestions are recorded but never become needs directly. Ask what goal or pain sits behind the suggestion, and code that too.

## From observations to insights

1. Lay out all observations, ignoring which interview they came from.
2. Group by underlying meaning. Two observations belong together if the same design change would address both.
3. Name each group with a full sentence, not a noun. If you cannot write the sentence, the group is not ready.
4. Count distinct participants per group, not observations. Five observations from one person is still n = 1.
5. For each group, search for disconfirming observations. Note them on the card.
6. Merge groups that lead to the same implication. Split groups whose members point to different implications.

## Topic versus insight

| Topic (not an insight) | Insight |
| --- | --- |
| Trust | Sole traders double-check every automated figure against their bank app because one past error cost them a late fee, so a number shown without its source gets ignored |
| Notifications | Shift managers mute all alerts in the first week because most are not about their site, so the one urgent alert arrives in a muted channel |
| Pricing confusion | Team leads cannot tell what a "seat" is before buying, so they underbuy and hit a wall on day one |

## Handling conflict and outliers

- Two clear camps: report both as separate insights with their counts. Look for what distinguishes the camps (segment, experience, context).
- A single striking participant: keep them as a signal, with the reason they stand out. Do not dissolve them into the average and do not promote them to a theme.
- Stated versus observed: when what someone said conflicts with what they did in the session, trust the behaviour and report the gap; the gap is often the finding.

## When you are done

Each insight should survive this question from a sceptical stakeholder: "Show me who said that." If you cannot point to quote IDs from at least two participants, it is a signal.
