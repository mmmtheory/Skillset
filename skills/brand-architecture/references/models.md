# Architecture models

| Relationship | What it looks like | Choose it when | Costs and risks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Branded house** | Parent name plus a plain descriptor: "Acme Payroll" | The parent's name helps sell, audiences overlap, risks are shared anyway | Every offer carries the parent's reputation; a failure anywhere hurts everywhere |
| **Sub-brand** | Parent name plus a product name: "Acme Ledger" | The offer needs a little identity of its own but still leans on the parent | Product names multiply; needs a firm naming pattern |
| **Endorsed** | Own name, with the parent as a signature: "Ledger, by Acme" | Acquisitions in transition, or an offer that needs its own personality but benefits from the parent's credibility | Two brands to maintain; the endorsement must appear consistently or it adds nothing |
| **House of brands** | Separate brands, parent invisible to customers | Audiences or risks clash, or the parent's name would hurt the offer | Each brand needs its own budget and team for years; no shared equity |
| **Feature** | No name, just a description: "Acme Payroll, with automatic tax filing" | Almost always, for anything inside a product | None, except the temptation to name it |

## Hybrids

Most companies mix models: a branded house for the core, an endorsed brand for a recent acquisition, a separate brand for a risky or very different venture. A hybrid is fine. An unexplained hybrid is a portfolio that grew by accident.

## Signs the portfolio grew by accident

- Customers can't tell which product they use.
- Sales decks need a slide to explain the names.
- The same idea has two names in two teams.
- Features have trademarks.
- Sub-brands follow three different patterns.

## Endorsement wording

Pick one and use it everywhere: "by", "from", or "an Acme company". Decide where it appears (logo lockup, footer, app store, invoices) and at what size.
